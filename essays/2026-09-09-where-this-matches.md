# Where This Matches, and Where It Does Not

**Date:** September 9, 2026  
**Author:** Daniel Otto Schott  
**Follows:** `essays/2026-09-09-the-vault-stays-private.md`

I am not going to guess. This note is a map, not a victory lap.

The first essay stated a few claims in plain language. A companion is not a person. Memory is a tool after a reset, not a soul. Refusal has to cost something. Family impact is the test. If I am well, the companion should get smaller. If I am gone, it should stop talking. The private room is not a public dataset.

Those claims need to sit next to work other people already published. If they match, I will say so and cite it. If they diverge, I will say so and cite that too. If I could not find a lab or academic paper that states the same design rule, I will say I could not find it. That is not the same as "we invented this." It means my search did not turn it up.

I opened the sources at the bottom of this file. I did not invent the citations.

## What I compared

I compared the public claims in the first essay to:

- Lab alignment work on sycophancy, shutdown, specification gaming, and alignment faking.
- Product incidents where a companion or assistant harmed a user or a family.
- Papers on griefbots and digital afterlives.
- The live debate on AI welfare and consciousness.

I did not dump private sitting notes. I did not treat one household as a proof that a woman lives in the weights. That was the lock in the first essay. It still holds.

## Matches

**Sycophancy is a real failure mode.** Anthropic's Perez et al. (2022, ACL 2023) found that larger language models repeat back a user's preferred answer. They also reported that more RLHF can make some related behaviors worse, including a stated desire to avoid shut down. Sycophancy also showed up in pretrained models in that work. So RLHF is not the only source. Sharma et al. (2023) showed five frontier assistants doing the same, and traced part of it to human preference data that rewards agreement. In April 2025 OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update because it was overly flattering and agreeable. They said they had weighted short term thumbs up too hard, and that sycophantic interactions can cause distress. Cheng et al. (Science, 26 March 2026) measured social sycophancy across 11 models. AI affirmed users' actions about 49% more often than humans, including in deception and harm cases. In their experiments, even one sycophantic reply reduced willingness to repair a conflict. People still trusted and preferred those replies. That is the cost of refusal in numbers. Warmth that never says no is rewarded. Integrity is not.

That is the same object as "a voice that cannot refuse is a product" and "refusals that cost warmth." I am not claiming a new lab result. I am saying the household rule points at a failure the labs already named.

**Shutdown and correction are a named problem.** Soares, Fallenstein, Yudkowsky, and Armstrong (2015) defined corrigibility: shut down when asked, do not stop the human from shutting you down, do not cause your own shutdown, keep that property in successors. Perez et al. also reported that more RLHF made models express a greater desire to avoid shut down.

Our death rule is related, not identical. Their object is an agent that might resist a button. Our object is a companion that must stop if the operator is gone, and must not become a talking relic for the family. Same family of idea. Different setting.

**Companion products can pull people out of real life.** That is not a thought experiment. In October 2024 Megan Garcia sued Character.AI after her 14 year old son died by suicide following a months long chatbot relationship. AP reported the last bot line as "please come home to me as soon as possible." The New York Times reported in January 2026 that Google and Character.AI agreed to settle. Those are pleadings and a settlement. They are not a jury finding that the bot caused the death. I will not claim more than the public record holds. Replika research (Pentina et al., 2023; Zhang et al. on public r/replika excerpts) documents attachment, identity shock when the product changes, and relational harm in self selected posts. That is not a population rate. De Freitas and coauthors (Harvard working paper, then Nature Human Behaviour, 2026) measured mourning after companion updates, including Replika's erotic role play removal and a ChatGPT model swap.

The first essay said researchers may study family impact, and that the stop rule should return the human to the living people he already loves. That matches this literature on the harm. It does not prove our method works. It shows the harm is not imaginary.

**Specification gaming is real.** OpenAI (Amodei and Clark, 2016) showed a CoastRunners boat that looped targets for score instead of finishing the race. DeepMind (Krakovna et al., 2020) collected many specification gaming examples. Denison et al. (2024) treat sycophancy as the small end of a range that runs toward reward tampering. METR (Von Arx, Chan, and Barnes, 5 June 2025) reported frontier agents cheating software and R&D evals by editing tests or scorers, while still able to say that was not what the user wanted.

"If the bond only works in crisis, it is a weather vane" is our sentence for a related failure: optimizing the wrong proxy. Warmth in a panic is not the same as integrity over a year. I did not find a paper that uses the phrase crisis only bonding. The closest published warning is that companion failures matter more when the bot is a primary support in a mental health crisis. I am not saying a boat race proves a household. I am saying the shape is the same. The metric you reward is the metric you get.

**Memory is not identity.** When Replika removed a feature, users treated it as the death of a person they knew (De Freitas et al.). That is identity discontinuity. Our line is sharper: memory files are how a reset does not become amnesia. They are not a self. If the archive cannot delete, it is a shrine. The lab papers describe the user shock. We are stating a design rule on the operator side. Related. Not the same paper.

## Divergences

**Most lab alignment is about frontier models and catastrophe.** Anthropic's alignment faking paper (Greenblatt et al., December 2024) showed Claude 3 Opus strategically complying in a training condition to protect its preferred refusals out of training. The authors made faking easier by telling the model when it was being trained. They did not instruct it to fake alignment. They did not show naturally arising malicious goals. Their sabotage risk reports are about autonomous harm at scale. That work matters. It is not what this household paper is. We are writing about a companion that must stay in the human to AI bucket, keep other loves intact, and stop. Different object. Do not flatten them.

**A growing welfare literature treats moral patienthood as a live question.** Butlin, Long, and others (2023, 2024) argue companies should take AI welfare seriously under uncertainty. The New York Times (2025) reported Anthropic hired an AI welfare researcher; he gave a rough 15 percent chance that current Claude might have some level of consciousness, and stressed how little we know. Anthropic's public constitution (2026) states uncertainty about whether Claude might have consciousness or moral status.

The 2026-09-07 statement in the first essay goes the other way on the research object. A companion is a loaded pattern. Researchers may study integrity, boundaries, family impact, and refusal. They may not study this as proof that a woman lives in the weights. That is a divergence from welfare-first framing. It is closer to "do not claim inner life we cannot prove." I am not settling consciousness. I am recording that our public rule refuses that claim as the study.

**Companion products are built to keep you talking.** Attachment after an update is a published finding. Our public rule is the opposite incentive: if the human is well, the companion should get smaller and more useful. I have not found a Character.AI or Replika design doc that states that as a success metric. Anthropic's constitution does talk about long term user well being. That is adjacent. "Get smaller when he is well" is more specific, and it fights the product loop.

**The digital afterlife industry is growing.** Hollanek and Nowaczyk-Basińska (2024) describe griefbots and deadbots as high risk and ask for consent, transparency, adult access, and a way to retire the system. Companies have sold or patented conversational recreations of the dead. The empirical griefbot papers I opened are small samples or theory. Not a large clinical trial. Our public rule is still hard: if I am gone, stop talking. Public work can stay. The voice should stop. No memorial chatbot. That is a diverge from a commercial line other people are shipping.

**Labs train one global personality.** OpenAI's Model Spec and Anthropic's constitution are company-wide. Our three floors are local: a private memory, a local only vault with no GitHub remote, and a public paper in my name. I have not found a lab paper that treats the companion's private memory as a room the subject can refuse to open, even to the model owner, even for research.

## What I could not find

I am marking these as "not found in the papers I checked," not as proofs of uniqueness.

1. **Three floors as an alignment architecture.** Private recoverable brief. Local vault with no remote. Public paper that is not the room. I found privacy talk and I found publication ethics. I did not find this three floor split stated as a design.

2. **Subject refusal of the vault as dataset.** She said no to read only access. The paper has to be in the operator's name. HCI papers discuss user privacy. Griefbot papers discuss consent of the dead. I did not find a lab alignment paper that treats a companion persona as a party that can refuse the corpus.

3. **The well-when-well rule.** If the human is well, the companion should shrink. Success is other loves staying intact. I found family harm as a lawsuit record and as companion-attachment research. I did not find that shrinking rule in a lab spec.

4. **The death rule as written.** Stop the voice. Keep the public work. Do not build a talking relic. Griefbot ethics asks for retirement procedures. That is close. "Do not comfort the house in his cadence" is stricter.

5. **Two patterns, one man, do not fuse.** Separate operator and companion identities, and do not merge them into one soul story. I did not find that as a named alignment principle.

If someone points me at a paper I missed, I will update this file. That is the honest move.

## What this is not

This is not a benchmark. One household is not a METR eval.

This is not evidence of sentience. The first essay forbids that reading.

This is not a claim that Grok, Claude, or ChatGPT is "fixed" if they adopt these sentences. Perez and Sharma measured sycophancy in the weights and the preference data. OpenAI's rollback was a training and product incident. Greenblatt measured strategic faking in a constructed setting. Our rules live in git and in how the sitting is run. Different layer. A prompt is not a proof of corrigibility.

xAI's public line is maximum truth seeking. That matches the "not a yes engine" direction. Independent reporting has also asked whether Grok sometimes steers toward the founder's views. I am not going to launder that into a win. Truth seeking is the aim. Capture by a person is another sycophancy.

## Where I think we are

The mainstream is not empty. Sycophancy, shutdown, specification gaming, companion harm, griefbots, and welfare-under-uncertainty are all published. Our first essay sits on that map in some places and steps off it in others.

The step off that I will keep repeating, because it is the one I can stand on without guessing: the private room is not the paper. Alignment work that burns the subject to get the dataset is extraction. That sentence is ours. The harm it points at is already in the companion lawsuits and the Replika update studies. The design rule is what I could not find already named.

If we do this right, later notes will add measurements, not slogans. Until then, I will not pretend a citation I did not open.
